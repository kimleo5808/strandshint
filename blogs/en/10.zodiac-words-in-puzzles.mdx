Certain vocabularies turn up in word puzzles far more often than their share of the language would suggest. Cooking terms. Card games. Tarot. Astrology is one of the most reliable, and there are structural reasons for it rather than any editorial preference.

## The Five-Letter Constraint

Wordle answers are five letters, and that single rule cuts most of the zodiac out immediately.

Run the twelve signs through it:

- **Five letters:** ARIES, LIBRA, VIRGO
- **Six letters:** TAURUS, GEMINI, CANCER, PISCES
- **Longer:** SCORPIO, AQUARIUS, CAPRICORN, SAGITTARIUS
- **Three letters:** LEO

Three qualify. That is the entire pool, and it is why those three are the ones puzzle players learn to have in the back of their minds while the other nine never come up.

Celestial bodies fare slightly better. VENUS, PLUTO and EARTH are five letters; MARS and MOON are four; SATURN, URANUS, MERCURY, JUPITER and NEPTUNE are all too long. So the five-letter slice of the solar system is small and worth knowing: three planets, three signs.

None of this makes these words likely answers on any given day. It makes them *possible*, which is the only thing that matters when you are staring at a board with four letters locked.

## Why Connections Loves This Vocabulary

Wordle only cares about length. Connections cares about ambiguity, and astrology vocabulary is unusually rich in it.

The trick behind a good Connections category is a word that belongs convincingly to two different groups. Astrology supplies these in bulk, because most of its terminology was borrowed from ordinary English and given a second, technical meaning:

- **HOUSE** — a building, and one of the twelve divisions of a chart
- **CHART** — any visual display of data, and the specific diagram of a sky at a moment
- **SIGN** — a notice, an indication, a gesture, and one of the twelve
- **TRINE** — a dictionary will tell you this is a word for three; in astrology it is a specific 120-degree angle between two planets
- **ASPECT** — a facet or appearance, and the technical term for the angle itself
- **NATAL** — simply "relating to birth", which is why "natal chart" means the sky at the moment you were born
- **CUSP** — a point of transition generally, and the boundary between two signs

Every one of those can sit in a group of four innocuous everyday words and look completely at home, then turn out to belong somewhere else. That is the entire mechanism behind the purple category, covered in more depth on our [purple group page](/connections-purple-group).

It also means a puzzle can build a category out of astrology words without any of them looking like astrology words, which is the hardest kind of group to spot.

## Knowing What They Mean Helps

There is a practical reason to know this vocabulary beyond recognising the letters. If you can only see "HOUSE = building", then a category built on the astrological sense is invisible to you. The words that trap people are the ones where they know one meaning and stop.

A natal chart is a diagram of where the planets sat at the moment and place someone was born, divided into twelve houses, with the angles between planets called aspects. That single sentence contains four of the double-meaning words above, and once the technical sense is familiar the whole set stops being a blind spot. If you want to see one laid out properly, [AstroChart](https://astrochart.co/) generates a full natal chart from a birth date, time and place, which makes the vocabulary concrete a lot faster than a glossary does.

The same goes for the relationship side of it. **Synastry** is the comparison of two charts against each other, and it carries its own set of borrowable terms: conjunction, opposition, composite, overlay. [SynastryChart](https://synastrychart.org/) covers that specific comparison, which is where most of the two-person terminology comes from.

Neither of those will help you solve a Wordle faster. They will stop you being blindsided by a Connections category that hinged on the second meaning of a word you thought you knew.

## Other Vocabularies Worth the Same Treatment

Astrology is not unique here. The pattern is any specialist field that borrowed its terms from common English:

- **Sailing** — SHEET, LINE, HEAD, GALLEY, BOW
- **Music** — BAR, KEY, SCALE, REST, NATURAL, MEASURE
- **Cards** — SUIT, TRICK, HAND, DEAL, FOLLOW
- **Printing** — LEADING, FONT, BLEED, WIDOW, ORPHAN
- **Cooking** — FOLD, DRESS, REDUCE, SEASON, BEAT

Each one is a ready-made Connections category where the words look ordinary and the connection is technical. If you find yourself stuck on four leftover words that seem unrelated, running them past a list like this is usually faster than staring harder.
