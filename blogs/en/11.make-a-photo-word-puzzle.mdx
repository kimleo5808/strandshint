**PIN.** Across the top are a rolling **pin** and a bowling **pin**. Across the bottom are a safety **pin** and a push**pin**. That last one is easy to call a *thumbtack*. Sometimes the move that solves a word puzzle is finding a second name for something you already recognize.

## Build a round for a friend

Work backward from a word you can picture in several places. Take *ring*. A key ring from the hall, a napkin ring from the table, a shower curtain ring from the bathroom and an onion ring from lunch would make a whole round. A bell is a tempting fifth clue. It can *ring*, but the other four have *ring* in their names. Save the bell for a round with a different rule.

Photograph each object on its own. Let the handle, clasp or other telling detail fit inside the frame. In the puzzle above, the little metal point on the blue tack earns its space. Cover it with your thumb and the plastic top starts to look like a board-game piece.

## Turn the photos into a playable grid

1. **Crop while the objects are still separate.** Give each photo the same shape and leave a little space around the edges. A tiny object can fill its square just as comfortably as a large one.
2. **Check what your collage editor accepts.** If an iPhone photo ends in `.heic` and the editor won't open it, select the file in [HEICHub's HEIC to PNG converter](https://heichub.com/), choose **Convert to PNG**, then download the PNG and add it to the grid. You can skip this step for photos the editor already opens.
3. **Give the grid a test solve.** Put the four images in two rows, shrink the collage to chat-preview size and ask one person to name what they see. If they say “keys” when you meant “key ring,” retake that shot with one key hanging from a clearly visible ring.

Send the grid and ask, “What word appears in all four names?” Keep the answer for a second message so everyone gets a turn. If you enjoy the moment when four ordinary things suddenly fit together, try the word-based groups in our [Connections solving guide](/blog/how-to-solve-connections).
